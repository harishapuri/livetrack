# How to file this draft in India

**DRAFT CHECKLIST.** This folder is not a filed application and not legal advice. A registered Indian patent agent should review every form, run a prior-art search, and e-file. Nothing in this folder has been submitted to the Patent Office.

India has no separate utility-model patent. This package is an ordinary complete specification under the Patents Act, 1970.

## What you must fill in before filing

Leave no `[TO FILL]` blank in the forms you actually upload.

- Applicant legal name, full postal address, nationality, and category: natural person, startup, small entity, or other. A startup or small entity that wants the reduced fee also needs the evidence the Office requires on that day (often Form 28 and, for a startup, DPIIT recognition).
- Inventor legal name, address, and nationality. If the applicant is a company and the inventor is a person, they are not the same party. Form 1 then needs the basis on which the applicant has the right to apply.
- Address for service in India.
- The Patent Office: Delhi, Mumbai, Chennai, or Kolkata, from the table below.
- Signature and date on Form 1, Form 2, Form 3, and Form 5.
- Confirmation that no corresponding application has been filed outside India. If one has, replace the default statement in Form 3 and keep it updated until grant.

## Which Patent Office

The appropriate office follows the address for service, or the first applicant’s address when there is no separate address for service. Confirm the current territorial list on ipindia.gov.in on the filing day. The usual division is:

- **Delhi:** Haryana, Himachal Pradesh, Jammu and Kashmir, Ladakh, Punjab, Rajasthan, Uttar Pradesh, Uttarakhand, Delhi, and Chandigarh.
- **Mumbai:** Maharashtra, Gujarat, Madhya Pradesh, Goa, Chhattisgarh, and the Union territories of Daman and Diu and Dadra and Nagar Haveli.
- **Chennai:** Andhra Pradesh, Karnataka, Kerala, Tamil Nadu, Telangana, Puducherry, and Lakshadweep.
- **Kolkata:** the remaining states and union territories.

## E-filing order

1. Create an account on the IP India e-filing portal and confirm the fee schedule (Schedule I) for your applicant category that day.
2. Start an ordinary application with a **complete** specification. This folder is not a provisional.
3. Upload Form 1, Form 2, the abstract, the drawings, Form 3, and Form 5. Pay the filing fee, including any extra page fee and extra claim fee. The draft aims at **ten claims**. Recount pages after conversion to the Office layout (typically A4 with the prescribed margins). Markdown length is not the official page count.
4. Keep the filing receipt and the application number. Write that number onto Form 5 and, later, Form 18.
5. Form 18 (request for examination) is due within the statutory period, commonly **48 months from the filing date** for an application with no earlier priority. Confirm the period on the filing day. Examination does not start until Form 18 and its fee are received.
6. If you file outside India later, update Form 3. If you disclose the invention in public before filing, the filing date may be lost. File before any public demo, sale, or upload of this specification.

## After filing

The Office allots an application number if the papers and fee are in order. That is a filing, not a grant. Examination produces a report. Software and business-method objections are expected; see `08-divisionals-and-section-3k.md`. Publication makes the specification public. Do not put passwords, tokens, customer procedures, or prompt text into the filed papers. This draft does not include them.

## Claim count

Claims 1 to 10 in Form 2 are the set written for the base fee band. Claims 11 to 14 in the annex are optional. Do not paste the annex into the filed specification unless those claims are worth the fee and still belong to the same invention.
